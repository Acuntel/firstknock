# FirstKnock — Expansion Map: More Buyers, More Data Products

The core insight: every county adapter we build emits events that MULTIPLE industries pay for, and most of the buyers below already pay somebody for worse versions. This doc maps who else values the data and what new sellable products fall out of the same engine. Rule of thumb for prioritizing: prefer buyers served by adapters we already build for the 5 design partners (zero marginal data cost), who already pay for leads today.

## 2. New data PRODUCTS we could create and sell

Beyond per-seat SaaS digests, the same engine emits sellable artifacts:

1. **The Surplus Funds Ledger** (per county, updated after every sale cycle): computed overages + former-owner contact trail. Sold per-county subscription to recovery firms, or per-record. Highest per-record value of anything we can make; MI and IL both have active surplus regimes. COMPLIANCE FLAG: several states cap finder's fees or require registration; sell the DATA, never act as the recovery agent.
2. **Verified Cash-Buyer Feed** (per metro): every no-mortgage purchase + repeat-buyer LLCs, monthly. Wholesalers pay for dispo lists constantly; ours is deed-verified rather than scraped.
3. **Tired Landlord Index**: owners ranked by eviction frequency + code violations + absentee status. Sell to investors (acquisition) AND property managers (services) from one dataset.
4. **New Business Radar** (statewide weekly): new LLCs/licenses enriched with industry + address + registered agent. The IL SOS parser we already own makes this nearly free to launch. Sell as $99-299/mo per territory to B2B reps.
5. **Contractor Activity + Lien Risk Report**: permits by contractor + mechanics liens, monthly, sold to building-material distributors' credit and sales teams. B2B pricing ($500+/mo) because the buyer is a company, not an operator. FCRA FLAG: fine for trade-credit/B2B use; never market it for consumer credit, tenant, or employment decisions.
6. **Metro Distress Index** (free, monthly, public): one-page ranking of ZIPs by composite distress. This is the SEO/PR engine, not a revenue product: every local journalist covering the foreclosure wave cites it, every backlink sells the SaaS. The Carrot lesson applied to data.
7. **One-off productized CSVs** (immediate revenue, before the SaaS even ships): "Cook County pre-foreclosure pack, this month, contacts included, $299." Low-touch, validates demand vertical by vertical, and every buyer is a warm SaaS prospect. This can fund the build.
8. **API / data licensing tier** (later): the adapter layer sold Shovels-style to other software companies, at daily freshness instead of biweekly. Only after the SaaS proves; becoming a data vendor first would burn the moat for pennies.

## 3. Priority read (what I'd actually do)

1. **Now, alongside V1**: One-off CSV packs (product #7) and the New Business Radar (#4, parser already built). Both produce revenue this month with zero new adapters, and CSV buyers become design partners.
2. **First expansion after the 5 partners are live**: Surplus Funds Ledger (#1) and Cash-Buyer Feed (#2). Same foreclosure/deed adapters, wildly different buyers, top-tier willingness to pay.
3. **First new adapter to build for expansion**: eviction filings. One adapter lights up tired-landlord (investors AND PMs) and strengthens the investor preset.
4. **The sleeper B2B play**: Contractor Activity + Lien Risk (#5). Different buyer class (distributors), Levelset-precedent economics, and nobody is doing it fresh and local.
5. **Never rush**: agent presets (crowded), govtech (slow), API licensing (moat-burning if early).
